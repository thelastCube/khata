# steps to financial freedom

- Figure out your in-hand income every month, calculate your monthly expenditure and divide it into needs, wants, and savings.
    - *Needs* are your fixed expenses like rent / internet / utilities / any memberships or subscriptions / estimate of food & commute.
    - *Wants* are things you can live without but are nice-to-haves because life is not supposed to be… lifeless, these are money spent on your hobbies, travel, outings, etc.
    - *Savings* are for your emergency fund and investments.
    - Aim to save *at least* 20% of your income as savings. A good rule-of-thumb is the 50-30-20 rule (division of need-want-savings)
    - You don’t save money by micromanaging and microbargaining, you save by not spending more in your expenses.
- Build an emergency fund first — this is the money which you can live off of when you don’t have an income. Calculate your expenses (needs + wants) of a *basic lifestyle* for a year (conservative but job market is fucked rn). Put your emergency fund in FDs, it marginally beats inflation but the point is for it to be accessible in an 🥁🥁🥁 emergency.
  - split the fund into staggering ladders (1/2/3 yr or 1L/2L/3L) instead of one big FD
  - need to break one? break the newest / smallest with that covers the cost. This way you'll lose the least interest 
- The rest of your savings go to investments — put the money in index funds using SIP and forget about it for 7-10 years.
    - SIP will just give you a structure to follow so that you *do* invest a certain amount monthly.
    - Index funds reflect the overall market growth and making sure you don’t touch the money for a decade ensures that it is not too volatile to market recessions or that you don’t panic and fuck everything up with micromanaging your investments.
    - Short term investment is too volatile, and Intraday / micromanaging your portfolio is not worth the marginal gains given the amount of time you khapat in it.
    - Treat your money, once invested, as money vanished to the void of nothingness. ***DO NOT*** be dependent on it in any manner, truly forget about it. (except for remembering to invest)
    - Diversify your investments. Index funds by virtue are diversified, but you can look into investing in different index funds from different stock exchanges.
    - Talk to a CA to see how you can best make use of tax benefits (if any in tax regime)
    - Kill any high interest debt / loans first before investing a single gandhi. No index fund beats a 30% interest credit card. Low interest loans are fine.  
- Calculate a moving retirement fund amount. How much money would you need to not work another day?
    - are you considering an incrase in lifestyle? additional dependants (family)? any health expenses?
    - make a reasonable guess on how long you will live, how much paisa you need yearly for your desired lifestyle, calculate the money needed *now* adjusting inflation (take median of past 10-20 years). That’s how much you need *now* to just leave working for good.
    - this does not take into account your investments increasing in value in the future, consider that just a bonus
    - (per kingkong) A good rule-of-thumb is to have 30-40x of your annual expenses, if you want to be super safe then 50x. So if I’m happy with a 6 LPA life, then I need ~2.5-3Cr
    - This is just an educated guess. More of a precaution. Real life is always messy and things can fuck up real quick.
    - If you plan to move out, target 30-40x number to be in destination currency not INR
    - [Give this a read](https://www.mrmoneymustache.com/2012/01/13/the-shockingly-simple-math-behind-early-retirement/)

# budgeting kaise kare

- Identify the montly expenditure and make funds for it
    - Rent fund
    - Utilities fund (Electricity / Water / Internet)
    - Meal fund — groceries and shit
    - EMIs
    - Memberships / Subscriptions — Gym memberships, OTT subscriptions, VPN renewals, Google One, YT Music, etc if any
    - Travel / Commute — if you dont get travel allowance, which kinda sucks
    - Shopping — online or otherwise
    - Outings — bahar ka khana, cab, ghumna wagerah
    - Hobbies fund — for your books and watches and art supplies and workshops, all things hobbies, can divide this one further for specific hobbies
    - Charity fund
- Identify the yearly expenditure
    - Medical fund — for any and all non-insurance visits to the hospitals or medicine wagerah
    - Yearly Subscriptions — domain names, yearly subscriptions
    - Clothes — you’re not gonna buy them every month, just assign a yearly fund and don’t exceed it
    - Travel / Trips — budget for trips including *all expenses* and / or flights back home
    - Gift fund — birthdays and anniversaries are an inescapable truth of life
- Allot a budget, a calculated sum, for these funds and try not to exceed that. Any remaining money rolls over to the next month, or you can allot them to any particular fund(s) you want for the next month. If you didn’t spend that much on shopping this month and would rather want whatever’s remaining to go towards the Travel fund.
- make an easy way to record daily expenditure on the fly — could be an excel sheet, could be a simple mobile app frontend for an excel sheet, basically use excel for this
- If you exceed in a fund, try to adjust it in another fund. You can also siphon off one category for another. You can choose to give up money you kept aside for hobbies if you want to extend the charity fund for this month.
- Make a separate savings account for important categories, by themselves or combined — a separate bank account for trip fund, a separate bank account for the *needs* and a separate one for the *wants* (shopping, outings, hobbies)
- As you grow your income and your parents get older in the near future, start a *send money back home* fund. Check in with how much they need, provide that much and then some.
- Have a *near future* fund for money you'll need in 1-5 years - that laptop, trip, moving cities cushion. Can park this in FD too, boring an inaccessible (which is the point)
- This is just a guideline, a system to trust and follow instead of thinking in-the-moment every single time about financial decisions. It’s *fineeee* if you falter thoda idhar udhar, just get back on track. It’s not set in stone.

# insurance zaruri hota hai

- Health coverage is the non negotiable, do it before optimising anything else. One serious visit to the hospital and you are broke again, rather pay a thousand each month from the get go and not nuke everything. Company one won't cut it, it's barebones 

- Term insurance is necessary if people depend on your income. Basically when you die, this one makes sure your dependents keep getting money. It's optional, opt in when there's a need or if there's a surplus after needs-wants-savings - but but but the premium locks to your current age / health, so buying young is cheaper. Don't sit on it for too long.
