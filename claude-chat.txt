  insurance — the shield before the sword

  - Health insurance is non-negotiable. Get this before you optimize anything

    wants + savings, or once someone actually depends on your income (parents

  - Emergency fund is now money. Retirement fund is decades away money. But life

  ---

  a note on the funds (reverse EMI, not budgeting theatre)

  - The point of all these funds is reverse EMI — instead of buying now and
    paying later with interest, you pay yourself monthly and buy in full when
    the fund hits the number. You get the item and the interest, not the bank.
  - The EMI fund is the one exception and it's optional — it's for the rare case
    where you need something non-optional (say a laptop) that you'd rather not
    drop full amount on, and there's a genuine no-cost EMI. For those, and only
    those. If there's no no-cost EMI, reverse-EMI it instead.

  ---

  FD strategy — ladder it

  - Don't dump the emergency / FD money into one big fixed deposit. Ladder it —
    split into parts on staggered cycles (e.g. 1 / 2 / 3 year maturities, or 1L
    / 2L / 3L chunks).
  - When you need to break one, break the one farthest from maturing (least
    interest lost) or the smallest amount that covers the need — so you don't
    break a big fat FD for a small emergency and lose all its interest.
  - FDs barely beat inflation and that's fine — this money's job is safety and
    access, not growth. A sweep-in FD or liquid fund gives near-FD returns with
    instant access if you want the flexibility.

  ---

  - Leaning toward the higher end is defensible right now because the job market

    takes longer than it used to. More runway = more spine to walk away from a
    bad job.
  - Keep this as a live decision, not a fixed rule — 6, 12, or somewhere in
    between depending on how stable your income feels that year. Re-check it
    when your situation changes.

  ---

  - No de-risking, no shifting to "safe" assets as decades pass. The whole

    invested money, I don't panic-sell, I just ride it through. My needs are
    already covered by the safe buckets, so a crash is just a number on a

    the dip — so the rule is simply: don't.

  Layering (the one rule that governs everything)

  api → services → dao → sqlite. Routers are thin (parse, call a service,
  return). Services hold all logic (budget math, carryover, siphon) and never
  write SQL. Only DAOs touch the DB. DAOs return domain objects, not raw rows.

  Repo layout

  budget/
    backend/
      app/
        main.py           # app wiring, router mounts, auth dep
        config.py         # env: SECRET, DB_PATH, BACKUP_REPO
        db.py             # connection + schema init/migrations
        auth.py           # single-user auth dependency
        schemas.py        # pydantic request/response DTOs
        models.py         # domain models (plain dataclasses)
        dao/              # ONLY layer with SQL
          funds_dao.py  expenses_dao.py  labels_dao.py
          budgets_dao.py  transfers_dao.py  audit_dao.py
        services/         # logic; call DAOs, no SQL
          expense_service.py  budget_service.py  label_service.py
          analysis_service.py  audit_service.py  backup_service.py
        api/              # thin routers
          expenses.py  funds.py  budgets.py  labels.py  analysis.py  audit.py
      tests/
      pyproject.toml
    frontend/             # Vite PWA (see note)
    data/                 # sqlite file + csv exports

  SOLID pragmatically: DAOs are the interfaces services depend on (swappable,
  testable), services are single-responsibility. I won't add abstract base
  classes or a DI framework — plain constructor/function injection where it
  earns its keep.

  Data model (SQLite)

  - funds(id, name, color, sort, active)
  - budgets(fund_id, month YYYY-MM, amount, carry_underspend, carry_overspend,
    emi_months) — one row per fund per active month
  - expenses(id, ts, amount, fund_id, note) — one fund per expense
  - labels(id, name, color) · expense_labels(expense_id, label_id) —

  - groups(id, name) · group_labels(group_id, label_id)
  - transfers(id, month, from_fund_id, to_fund_id, amount, reason,

  - carry_adjustments(id, fund_id, month, amount, source_month, kind,
    emi_remaining) — opening adjustments the engine writes; EMI schedule lives

  - audit_log(id, ts, action, entity, entity_id, detail_json) — powers the

⏺ All four done. Here's the rundown.

  What khata is

  A single-user ledger for your money. You set a monthly budget per fund, log
  expenses as they happen, and khata shows you which funds are over, by how
  much, and how to rebalance. Everything's traceable.

  The mental model — three things:
  - Funds = your money buckets, each with a monthly budget (Rent, Food,
    Hobbies…). Every expense is charged to exactly one fund.
  - Labels = free tags you stick on expenses (groceries, friend, trip-goa). Any
    number per expense. These cut across funds — that's how you answer "how much
    on dining-out this month?" or "how much on that friend's visit?"
  - Groups = saved sets of labels, for quick filtering.

  The sections (top nav)

  - overview — your home screen. Funds sorted most-exceeded first, each with a
    spend bar and pills: budget (what you set), exceeded in red, rolled in / 
    repaying (carryover) in a darker shade. A close [month] button at the
    bottom.
  - add — fast expense entry: type an amount, pick a fund, type labels (new ones
    are created on the fly), date + note. That's the screen you'll use daily.
  - fund detail (tap any fund) — every expense with timestamps + labels, spend
    vs budget, delta vs last month, a "balance this overage" button, and a
    traceability list showing siphons and carryover.
  - analyse — pick any labels (matches expenses having all of them), a group, a
    fund; get the total, count, and breakdowns by fund and label.
  - settings — manage funds + their per-month budgets and the two carry toggles,
    labels, groups, the body font, and CSV backup.
  - log — a plain-English audit timeline of everything that happened.

  How you'd actually use it

  1. Settings → add your funds, and for each set this month's budget. Flip carry
     toggles where you want them (see below).
  2. Add an expense whenever you spend. Tag it.
  3. Overview to see where you stand; tap a fund for detail.
  4. If a fund's red, open it → balance this overage → khata suggests funds with
     spare budget and you siphon from one. Logged.
  5. End of month → close [month]. This applies carryover per your toggles.

  The two carry toggles (per fund, off by default):
  - roll leftover into next month — underspend carries forward.
  - shrink next month by overspend — you "repay" an overspend, optionally spread
    EMI-style over N months.
